{{sn}}Truss roof: analyse, check and present

By the end you have analysed one roof truss, checked it three ways, and written down what you found.

Idea

A roof load is an area load q in kN/m². One inner truss collects a strip as wide as the truss spacing, so its line load is w = q × s, and each top node takes P = w L / n. Analyse one truss in its plane before you try the whole roof. It is quicker to check and tells you nearly everything.

Try it here

In Rhino

  1. Add Karamba to the truss as in Session {{n:s17}}. Compute P from q, s, L and n with Multiplication and Division, so the load follows the sliders.
  2. Check 1, statics: the chord force against M / H.
  3. Check 2, stress: your own 検定比 for the most loaded tension member.
  4. Check 3, stiffness: midspan deflection against your limit. A common office target for steel roof members is span / 300. Confirm the one your project uses.
  5. Move the depth slider from 1.6 m to 3.2 m in steps and note the chord force and deflection each time. Which depth would you propose, and why?
  6. Write half a page: the brief, a screenshot of the definition, a screenshot of the result, the three checks, your proposed depth.

Watch out: the 20-beam limit

A truss with 6 panels has 25 members, which is over the limit of the full-feature trial. Either drop to 4 panels (17 members), run in free mode and do the stress check by hand, or buy the student licence.

Take it further

  • Add the buckling check from Session {{n:m1}} to the compression members, and let Galapagos from Session {{n:o1}} choose the depth.
  • Fill the comparison table below with three depths you tried, to practise for the projects that follow.

{{sn}}Footbridge: three options

By the end you have one definition that draws three structural options for a park footbridge, switchable with one value list.

ProjectPark footbridge (歩道橋)
Span30 m, simply supported
Clear width3 m
Your roleOption study
Hand inEnd of session {{n:p1b}}

A city park needs a footbridge across a river, 30 m between abutments. The landscape architect asks which steel structure suits it best and wants three options compared on one page.

Requirements for this exercise
  • Pedestrian load 5 kN/m² on the deck, carried by two main trusses or arches, one on each side.
  • Deflection under that load no more than span / 500.
  • Nothing may stand more than 2.5 m above the deck, so the view is kept.
Options
  • A. Pratt truss
  • B. Warren truss
  • C. Tied arch with vertical hangers, as in the Session {{n:s13}} challenge, with its rise kept under 2.5 m

Steps

  1. Start from your truss definition (Session {{n:s9}}) and your tied arch (Session {{n:s13}} challenge). Put each in a named group.
  2. One Value List (Params, Input) with Pratt, Warren and Tied arch. Stream Filter picks which option's lines go on to the analysis.
  3. Deck: the bottom chord or tie carries cross beams every panel; the load on one main structure is 5 × 3 / 2 = 7.5 kN/m.
  4. Give every option the same inputs: span, depth or rise, and number of panels or hangers.

{{sn}}Atrium gridshell: form and panels

By the end you have three roof forms for a glazed courtyard, each with its panel flatness measured.

ProjectSchool courtyard roof (中庭)
Plan20 m × 20 m
SupportsAll four edges
Your roleForm and structure study
Hand inEnd of session {{n:p2b}}

A school wants to roof its courtyard with glass. The building around it can support the roof on all four edges. The architect wants a light steel grid, about 2 m by 2 m, and asks which form to take forward.

Requirements for this exercise
  • Rise between 2.5 m and 4 m.
  • Glass panels should be flat; the glass supplier accepts up to 5 mm of warp per panel, a figure set for this exercise.
  • Load for checking: 1.5 kN/m² over the plan, all members in compression under it if possible.
Options
  • A. Form-found net, flipped (Session {{n:k2}})
  • B. Spherical dome (Session {{n:f4}})
  • C. Translation dome (Session {{n:f4}} challenge)

Steps

  1. Build all three forms over the same 20 m square with the same 10 by 10 grid and the same rise.
  2. Measure every panel's warp with Plane Fit, and count the panels over 5 mm.
  3. For option A, use Galapagos from the Session {{n:k2}} challenge to hit the rise exactly.

Practice

Which option should give flat quad panels without any extra work? A. Form-found netB. Spherical domeC. Translation dome A translation surface has flat quads by construction. A form-found net generally does not, and a sphere's quads warp more towards its edges.

{{sn}}Atrium gridshell: analyse and compare

By the end you have analysed the three forms and can explain why the form-found one works hardest for its weight.

Steps

  1. Karamba for each form: the grid as beams with one pipe size, pinned along all four edges, 1.5 kN/m² turned into node loads (load × the plan area around each node).
  2. Read the largest deflection, the largest 検定比 and how many members go into tension.
  3. Size the pipe so the worst member passes, and record the steel weight.
  4. Fill in the table and write your recommendation, including what the glass supplier needs to hear.

ProjectOffice tower, concept stage
Height30 storeys × 4 m
Plate30 m square
Your roleStructure and facade study
Hand inEnd of session {{n:p3b}}

The architect is choosing between a straight tower and two twisting ones, and wants the facade to open towards the park on the south side. You are asked what the twist does to the structure and to the facade.

Options
  • A. No twist
  • B. 45° twist
  • C. 90° twist
Requirements for this exercise
  • A diagrid on the perimeter, nodes every two floors.
  • Openings larger towards a park point on the south side, rationalised into at most 6 panel types.

Steps

  1. Tower and diagrid from Session {{n:f3}}, with the twist on a Value List of 0, 45 and 90.
  2. Facade panels between the diagrid nodes on the south face, openings from the attractor in Session {{n:f2}}, rationalised to 6 types.
  3. Count the different diagrid member lengths for each option, rounded to 10 mm. Fewer lengths mean simpler fabrication.

Practice

Which option should have the fewest different diagrid member lengths? A. No twistB. 45° twistC. 90° twist Without twist or taper, every bay of the diagrid repeats. Twisting changes the geometry from floor to floor.

{{sn}}Diagrid tower: analyse and compare

By the end you know how the twist changes the tower's lateral stiffness and its steel, with numbers for each option.

Steps

  1. Karamba: the diagrid members as beams with one pipe size, fixed at the base, floor plates as rigid rings (stiff beams around each node level).
  2. Lateral load: a horizontal point load at every node level. Use 100 kN per level as a placeholder for this exercise.
  3. Read the top sway and the storey drift angles; compare with 1/200.
  4. Record steel weight, top sway, member lengths and facade panel types for each option, then fill in the table.

{{sn}}A one-page portfolio sheet

By the end you have one A3 sheet that shows how you think, ready for an Arup or KKE application or interview.

Idea

An interviewer looks at a portfolio page for less than a minute. The page should show a question, a method, options, a comparison and a decision, in that order. Pick your strongest project, usually the footbridge or the gridshell.

Steps

  1. Clean the definition: inputs on the left, results on the right, every group named, no loose wires. Add a short Scribble (Params, Util) title to each group.
  2. Capture the canvas with File, Export Quick Image, and the Rhino views with ViewCaptureToFile, all at the same size.
  3. Lay out the sheet in the order above. Keep text short: a reviewer reads the headings and the numbers.
  4. Practise saying the page out loud in two minutes, in English and in Japanese.

Practice

What should a reviewer understand first from the sheet? Which plug-ins you usedThe question you were answeringHow many components the definition has The question frames everything else. Tools are a means; put them in the method box.
