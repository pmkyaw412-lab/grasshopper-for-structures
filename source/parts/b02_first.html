First definitions

Three sessions on the mechanics of the canvas: components, wires, sliders and panels. You finish with a portal frame whose span and height are two sliders.

{{sn}}Components, wires and sliders

By the end you can read any small definition from left to right and say what each box does.

Idea

A component takes data in on the left, does one job, and gives data out on the right. A Number Slider makes a number. A Panel, the yellow note, shows whatever you wire into it. Use panels constantly: when you are not sure what a wire carries, plug it into a panel and read it.

The colour of a component is its status, and this page uses the same code.

Example: a point and a line

Two sliders set the X and Z of point B. Point A has nothing wired in, so it stays at the origin. Line joins them, Length measures the result, and a panel shows the number.

Try it here

In Rhino

  1. Build the definition above. Find each component by double-clicking the canvas and typing its name.
  2. Double-click the name of a slider to open its settings. Set the range to 0 to 10 and the rounding to one decimal.
  3. Shortcut: double-click the canvas and type 0<6<12. You get a slider from 0 to 12 already set to 6.
  4. To unplug a wire, right-click the input and choose Disconnect. To plug a second wire into the same input, hold Shift while you drag.

{{sn}}A parametric portal frame

By the end you have a portal frame (門形ラーメン) driven by a span slider and a height slider.

Idea

One slider can feed many inputs. That is the whole point of a parametric model: the span is stored once, and every point that depends on it follows. Here the span L goes to the X of two points and the height H goes to the Z of two points.

Build frames in the XZ plane, which is the Front view. Z is up, and the analysis plug-in applies gravity along Z.

Example

Four points: A at the left base, B at the left top, C at the right top, D at the right base. Polyline joins them in the order you wire them. Explode breaks the polyline back into three separate members, because an analysis needs one line per member. Length then returns three numbers, one per segment.

Try it here

In Rhino

  1. Place four Construct Point components and two sliders. Wire L and H as in the figure. Leave Y empty on all four.
  2. Place Polyline. Wire A into V, then hold Shift and wire B, C and D into the same input, in that order.
  3. Add Explode and Length, and read the lengths in a panel.
  4. Select everything, press Cmd + G to group it, and type a name on the group: Portal frame geometry.

Watch out: wiring order is list order

When several wires enter one input, the data arrives in the order you connected them. Wire D before C and the polyline crosses itself. If a shape looks wrong, plug the input into a panel and check the order.

{{sn}}Rhino geometry in, baked geometry out

By the end you can bring a curve you drew in Rhino into a definition, and send Grasshopper's result back to Rhino on a layer.

Idea

Data crosses between the two windows in both directions. Going in, a Curve or Point parameter can reference an object in the Rhino model: move the object and the definition updates. Going out, Bake turns the red preview into real Rhino objects. Baked objects are a copy. They do not update when you move a slider afterwards.

At an office the usual pattern is: the architect's roof line or surface comes in by reference, your definition generates the structure on it, and you bake the result when a version is agreed.

Example

In Rhino

  1. In the Front viewport, draw a gentle roof line about 24 m long with Curve.
  2. In Grasshopper place a Curve parameter (Params, Geometry). Right-click it, choose Set one Curve, and click your roof line.
  3. Wire it into Divide Curve (Curve, Division) with a slider on N. Red points appear along the line.
  4. In Rhino, drag a control point of the roof line (turn them on with PointsOn). The points follow.
  5. Right-click Divide Curve, choose Bake, pick a layer, and confirm. The points are now real Rhino points.
  6. Right-click the Curve parameter and choose Internalise data. The curve now lives inside the .gh file and no longer needs the Rhino object.

Keep definitions readable from the first day: inputs on the left, logic in the middle, results on the right, every stage in a named group. Double-click a wire to add a Relay when a long wire needs a bend. A colleague should be able to follow it without you.
