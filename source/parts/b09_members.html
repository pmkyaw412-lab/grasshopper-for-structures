Member design

Analysis tells you the forces. Design tells you whether the members can carry them. These three sessions build the Japanese allowable stress checks (許容応力度設計) into Grasshopper: buckling, bending with axial force, and load combinations, ending in a check report you could hand to a senior engineer.

{{sn}}Column buckling

By the end you can work out a column's allowable compressive stress fc from its slenderness, and find the lightest section that carries a load.

Idea

A slender member in compression fails by buckling (座屈) sideways long before the steel yields. How slender it is depends on two things:

  • The buckling length lk (座屈長さ) = K × L, where K depends on how the ends are held. In theory K is 0.5 with both ends fixed, 0.7 with one fixed and one pinned, 1.0 with both pinned, and 2.0 for a cantilever.
  • The radius of gyration i (断面二次半径) of the section. An H-section has a large ix and a small iy, so unless it is braced it buckles about its weak axis.

The slenderness ratio (細長比) is λ = lk / i. Japanese practice for steel with F = 235 N/mm² then gives the long-term allowable compressive stress fc:

Critical slenderness (限界細長比)Λ = √(π²E / 0.6F) ≈ 120
When λ ≤ Λfc = {1 − 0.4(λ/Λ)²} F / ν, ν = 3/2 + 2/3 (λ/Λ)²
When λ > Λfc = 0.277 F / (λ/Λ)²
Short-term1.5 times the long-term value
Slenderness limitλ ≤ 200 for columns and λ ≤ 250 for other compression members (Building Standard Law Enforcement Order, Article 65)

Example

An H-250×250×9×14 column, 4 m tall, pinned at both ends, carries N = 600 kN long-term. iy = 6.32 cm and A = 91.43 cm².

  1. λ = 400 / 6.32 = 63.3, below Λ.
  2. λ/Λ = 0.528, so ν = 1.5 + 0.667 × 0.279 = 1.686.
  3. fc = (1 − 0.4 × 0.279) × 235 / 1.686 = 123.8 N/mm².
  4. σc = 600,000 / 9,143 = 65.6 N/mm², so the 検定比 is 65.6 / 123.8 = 0.53.

Try it here

In Rhino

  1. Keep a small section table in a panel or a CSV file read with Read File: name, A, ix, iy, Zx. Pick a row with List Item and split it into numbers with Text Split (Sets, Text).
  2. Buckling length: Multiplication of L and K. Slenderness: Division by iy (and by ix), then Maximum (Maths, Util) of the two.
  3. Two Expressions, one for each fc formula, with inputs λ, Λ and F.
  4. Larger Than compares λ with Λ. Pick'n'Choose (Sets, List) uses that result to pick the right formula: 0 picks the first stream, 1 the second.
  5. σc = N / A, then the ratio σc / fc. Show it in a panel, and colour the column with Custom Preview when it is over 1.

Watch out: theoretical K values

The K values above are the theoretical ones. Real ends are never perfectly fixed, so design guides often use larger values, and your office will have its own rule. In a moment frame, the column's buckling length is often taken as the storey height. Ask before you choose.

    {{sn}}Bending plus axial force

    By the end you can check a column that carries both axial force and bending, and say what the check assumes.

    Idea

    A frame column carries axial force from the floors above and bending from the frame action. The two stresses add up, so the check adds the two ratios (組合せ応力):

    Compression and bendingσc / fc + σb / fb ≤ 1
    σcN / A
    σbM / Z

    fb, the allowable bending stress, equals ft = F / 1.5 only when the compression flange cannot buckle sideways. An unbraced H-section can twist and buckle sideways under bending (横座屈), and the code then gives a smaller fb. Square hollow sections (角形鋼管) do not suffer this, and they are equally stiff in both directions. That is one reason Japanese moment-frame columns are usually square tubes.

    Try it here

    In Rhino

    1. Take the column forces from Karamba's Beam Forces: N and the moment M about the strong axis, at both ends of each element.
    2. σc = N / A and σb = M / Z with Division. Watch the units: kN and cm² give kN/cm²; multiply by 10 for N/mm².
    3. Reuse your fc from the last session. fb = 156 for a braced member.
    4. Add the two ratios and take the largest along the member with Sort List or Bounds.

    Practice

    {{sn}}Load combinations and the check report

    By the end your definition checks long-term and short-term combinations, drift and deflection, and writes a one-panel report.

    Idea

    Allowable stress design checks combinations of loads (荷重の組合せ), each against its own allowable stress:

    CombinationLoadsAllowable
    Long-term (長期)G + P: dead and live loadLong-term, such as F / 1.5
    Short-term (短期), earthquakeG + P + K and G + P − K1.5 × long-term
    Short-term, snow or windG + P + S, G + P + W1.5 × long-term

    K appears with both signs because an earthquake can come from either side. Snow rules differ in heavy-snow areas (多雪区域), so check the rules for your site. On top of the stress checks come the stiffness checks: storey drift angle under K, normally 1/200 or less, and beam deflection under long-term load, where many offices aim for span / 300.

    A linear analysis can be added up. Put G in load case 0 and K in load case 1, and the result of G + K is the sum of the two cases. You do not need to run the analysis again for each combination.

    Try it here

    In Rhino

    1. Give the vertical loads LCase 0 and the lateral loads LCase 1.
    2. Beam Forces once with LCase 0 and once with LCase 1. Combine them with Addition and Subtraction: G + K and G − K.
    3. Ratios for each combination. Divide the short-term ones by 1.5, or multiply the allowables.
    4. Drift: Nodal Displacements for LCase 1 at beam level, divided by the storey height.
    5. Report: Format (Sets, Text) builds a line such as Left column 0.71 0.93 OK. Merge the lines into one panel.

    Practice
